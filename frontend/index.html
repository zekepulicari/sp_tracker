    Plan a wildlife visit with evidence

    Species Tracker connects a place and a date with recorded wildlife sightings and environmental conditions. It helps you identify animals to look for and compare modeled conditions for a selected species.

    Three focused questions

    1. What animals have been recorded here in this season?
    2. How do conditions look for one animal?
    3. Which supported upcoming day has the most favorable modeled conditions?

    What the evidence can say

    Community sightings are observations, not surveys of every animal. More records can mean more observers, easier access, or easier identification. A classifier trained on sightings and background samples does not establish the probability that you will see an animal.

    This portfolio project demonstrates GIS, data ingestion, model validation, asynchronous APIs, and an accessible web interface. It does not provide navigation or field-safety advice.

    Documentation

    Statistical methods, data sources, and limitations of the current analyses. Historical Profile summarizes records; Environmental Outlook and Key Factors fit presence/background classifiers. None estimates the probability of a successful wildlife encounter.

    Shared data and predictors

    Single-species analyses use research-grade, georeferenced, non-captive iNaturalist observations from the preceding ten years ending yesterday. The study circle starts at 5 km and expands in 5 km steps until at least 50 usable location/date records are found, or the radius reaches 50 km. The requested sightings cap is 200 by default (100–500 allowed); capped samples are disclosed. Records are deduplicated by source ID, not by animal, so different records can describe the same individual.

    Environmental models label sightings as 1 and generated background samples as 0. One background point is generated per sighting before environmental exclusions, inside the same WGS84 geodesic circle, using seed 42. Background dates are sampled from the distinct sighting dates. These points represent available conditions, not verified absences; missing environmental data can change the final class balance. After selecting a discovery result, the circle is centered on a point inside the polygon and differs from the discovery extent.

    Environmental predictors and daily UTC aggregation
    SettingPredictors
    TerrestrialMean air temperature (°C), mean wind speed (km/h), total precipitation (mm/day).
    MarineMean sea surface temperature (°C), mean wind speed (km/h), mean wave height (m), mean ocean current velocity (km/h).

    Training and prediction use the same aggregation, requiring all 24 hourly values. Incomplete historical records are excluded; missing forecasts are unavailable rather than replaced with averages. Marine SST and current history is requested from January 2022 onward; archive weather has a five-day publication guard. Source resolution and coastal coverage can limit evidence.

    Both classifiers add cyclic month features: sin(2π(m − 1)/12) and cos(2π(m − 1)/12), where m is the calendar month. This keeps December adjacent to January. It represents a smooth annual seasonal component, without modeling a year-to-year trend or differences between days in the same month.

    Historical Profile · descriptive statistics

    For each month m, the count is n(m) = Σ I(month of record = m): the number of retrieved sightings in that month, summed across years. The chart also lists the distinct years represented. A zero means no matching record in this sample, not confirmed absence.

    No classifier is fitted and no weather variables enter this calculation. Counts are not divided by observer effort, visits, area surveyed, or the number of years. With a capped local sample, recent records can dominate; different months and locations may have unequal recording effort. The chart therefore describes recorded seasonality, not abundance, population trends, or the chance of seeing an animal. No confidence intervals or significance tests are calculated.

    Environmental Outlook · regularized logistic regression

    A binomial generalized linear model with a logit link distinguishes recorded-sighting conditions from sampled background conditions. Its score is s(x) = 1 / (1 + exp(−η)), where η = β₀ + Σ βⱼzⱼ and z contains the standardized environmental and seasonal predictors. Standardization subtracts each training predictor's mean and divides by its standard deviation; the fitted transformation is reused for prediction.

    Coefficients are fitted by minimizing logistic loss with L2 regularization (C = 1, up to 1,000 optimizer iterations). The penalty shrinks coefficients to reduce instability; it does not remove sampling bias. The model is additive and linear on the log-odds scale, with no explicit interaction or nonlinear environmental terms. Standardization is fitted only on the training portion of each validation split. Hyperparameters are fixed rather than tuned against the holdouts.

    The 0–1 score reflects the constructed sighting/background classification task. Its value depends on background sampling and class composition, so it is not a calibrated encounter probability. When validation permits an outlook, the forecast score is compared with the 25th and 75th percentiles of scores in the final training sample: at or above the upper quartile is “more favorable,” otherwise at or below the lower quartile is “less favorable,” and otherwise “typical.” These descriptive cutoffs are not confidence intervals or ecological thresholds.

    The final model uses all usable records after evaluation. A forecast with any environmental predictor outside its observed training minimum–maximum range is flagged, and a reliable favorability description is withheld. This range check cannot detect every unfamiliar combination of otherwise in-range variables. Coefficient uncertainty, p-values, and probability calibration are not computed.

    Key Factors · Random Forest

    A Random Forest classifier combines 200 decision trees fitted to bootstrap samples, with random subsets of predictors considered at each split. Trees have maximum depth 8 and at least 3 samples per leaf; seed 42 makes fitting repeatable. Unlike the logistic model, trees can capture threshold effects and interactions without specifying them in advance. This method describes historical associations rather than the selected day's forecast.

    The bars show normalized mean decrease in impurity: how much a predictor's splits reduce Gini impurity, weighted by the samples reaching those splits and averaged across trees. Values sum to approximately 100% after rounding. An importance of 30% means that share of the forest's attributed impurity reduction, not a 30% change in encounter chance or 30% of explained ecological variation.

    Importance gives no direction of effect: a large temperature bar does not say whether warmer conditions are better. Correlated variables can share or obscure importance, and variables with more possible split values may be favored. These are training-based importances, not held-out permutation importance or causal effects. The forest receives the same spatial and chronological validation as the logistic model; its bars alone do not establish predictive skill.

    Validation · testing environmental information against seasonality

    Models require at least 40 usable records overall, including at least 15 sightings and 15 backgrounds. Non-finite predictors and datasets with all environmental predictors constant are rejected. Each evaluated split needs at least 20 training records, 10 test records, and 3 records from each class in both portions. These minimums permit fitting; they do not establish adequate ecological evidence.

    • Spatial holdout: records in the same 0.1-degree latitude/longitude cell stay together across dates. Cell width varies with latitude. With at least four cells, approximately 25% of groups are held out. Up to eight seeded candidate splits are considered, using the first that meets class and sample requirements; this is one holdout, not an average of eight-fold cross-validation.
    • Chronological holdout: with at least ten distinct dates, the earliest roughly 80% of dates train the model and the latest roughly 20% test it. All records on the same date stay together. The fractions refer to distinct dates, not necessarily record counts. Locations may recur across this split; it tests later dates separately from spatial transfer.
    • Seasonal baseline: a regularized logistic classifier uses only month sine/cosine features on exactly the same training and test records. This checks whether environmental predictors add information beyond recorded seasonality.

    ROC AUC measures how well scores order sightings above backgrounds across thresholds; 0.5 indicates chance-level ordering and 1 indicates perfect separation of these test classes. Balanced accuracy averages sighting recall and background recall using a score threshold of 0.5. It gives the two classes equal weight even when exclusions leave unequal counts. Neither metric measures successful field visits.

    For each holdout, improvement requires strictly higher AUC than the seasonal baseline and balanced accuracy at least as high. Both holdouts must pass to permit favorability descriptions or day rankings. If either cannot be evaluated, the result is marked experimental. Diagnostics are single-split estimates without confidence intervals or statistical significance tests; a small numerical improvement is not proof of a robust ecological effect. Spatial grouping reduces some leakage but does not eliminate observer bias or spatial dependence.

    When should I go? · comparison using one fitted model

    The logistic model is reused across the next seven days starting today, keeping species, study extent, features, and environmental setting fixed. Supported days are ordered by descending classifier score, with date breaking individual ties. A ranking requires both validation gates, at least two complete forecasts inside every environmental training range, and an overall score spread greater than 0.000001.

    “Most favorable” means highest relative score among the eligible days in this comparison. It does not establish that differences are statistically significant. No uncertainty intervals or pairwise significance tests are produced. Missing and out-of-range days are excluded; if a reliable ranking is unavailable, supported conditions are still shown. Environmental outlook dates outside the forecast window receive the historical profile with weather unavailable.

    Animals nearby · seasonal frequency ranking

    This is a descriptive ranking, not ten fitted environmental models. Complete research-grade, georeferenced, non-captive animal records are retrieved for the selected calendar month in the preceding ten years ending yesterday. Exact polygon filtering includes boundary points; subspecies consolidate under their species, and unresolved higher taxa are excluded.

    Species are ranked by observation count, then distinct observation dates, then ascending taxon ID. Up to ten are returned, including single-record species with an explicit label. There is no adjustment for detectability, observer effort, or differences between animal groups. The selected day affects weather context but not the same-month historical ranking. Weather is evaluated separately at a point inside the polygon.

    The complete bounding-box query must fit the configured cap (5,000 records by default), and pagination must finish successfully; otherwise the request fails rather than publishing a partial ranking. Sparse or empty results describe recorded evidence, not the absence of other animals.

    Geometry, evidence, and AI

    Shapely validates WGS84 polygons server-side; PyProj computes ellipsoidal distances and areas. Polygons require 3–100 vertices, no holes or crossing edges, and cannot cross the antimeridian. Result details disclose source timestamps and completeness; species analyses also retain source record identifiers, dates, exclusions, seed, feature definitions, and model version.

    Optional Gemini explanations summarize structured evidence and do not fit, select, or validate statistical models. The deterministic explanation remains available if AI fails. Interpret the diagnostics alongside sample coverage and exclusions; classifier scores are never encounter percentages.

    Statistical references: logistic regression, Random Forest feature importance, and classification metrics. The settings above describe this application's implementation.
