    Plan a wildlife visit with evidence

    Species Tracker connects a place and a date with recorded wildlife sightings and environmental conditions. It helps you identify animals to look for and compare modeled conditions for a selected species.

    Three focused questions

    1. What animals have been recorded here in this season?
    2. How do conditions look for one animal?
    3. Which supported upcoming day has the most favorable modeled conditions?

    What the evidence can say

    Community sightings are observations, not surveys of every animal. More records can mean more observers, easier access, or easier identification. A classifier trained on sightings and background samples does not establish the probability that you will see an animal.

    This portfolio project demonstrates GIS, data ingestion, model validation, asynchronous APIs, and an accessible web interface. It does not provide navigation or field-safety advice.

    Methods and limitations

    Animals nearby

    Research-grade, georeferenced wild-animal sightings are fetched from iNaturalist for the preceding ten years ending yesterday. Only the selected calendar month is included. Polygon filtering includes the boundary. Subspecies are consolidated into their species.

    Species are ordered by sighting count, then distinct observation dates, then taxon ID. Counts are complete for the requested query or the query fails with a request to narrow the area. A sparse or empty list is not evidence that other animals are absent.

    Historical profile

    A monthly chart summarizes a capped local sightings sample. It includes years represented and source dates, and does not estimate successful visits. The study radius expands from 5 km up to 50 km when sightings are sparse.

    Environmental analysis

    Terrestrial analysis uses daily mean air temperature and wind speed, daily total precipitation, and sine/cosine seasonality. Marine analysis uses daily mean sea surface temperature, wind speed, waves, ocean current velocity, and the same cyclic seasonality. Hourly UTC data is aggregated identically for training and forecasts; incomplete days are excluded.

    Background samples use a fixed seed and remain inside the same WGS84 geodesic study circle. They represent environmental availability, not confirmed absences. Regularized logistic regression provides an experimental outlook; Random Forest shows modeled associations through feature importance.

    Validation and day comparisons

    Spatial groups are approximately 10 km cells kept intact across dates. A separate chronological holdout uses the latest dates where sample sizes permit. Each evaluation compares against a cyclic seasonality-only classifier. A day ranking requires both holdouts to outperform that baseline, at least two complete dates inside the observed training ranges, and differing model scores.

    Days with missing data or conditions outside the training range are not ranked. Forecast coverage varies by source; this release requests at most seven days, using only supported returned data. Dates outside the forecast window show the historical profile without synthetic weather.

    Geometry, sources, and AI

    WGS84 coordinates are validated server-side with Shapely. PyProj computes ellipsoidal distances and areas. Polygons must have 3–100 vertices, no holes, no crossing edges, and cannot cross the antimeridian.

    Marine SST and currents are requested only from January 2022 onward; archive weather has a several-day delay. Data resolution and coastal coverage can limit conclusions. Every result records completeness, timestamps, excluded records, model version, and source identifiers.

    Optional Gemini summaries use supplied evidence; model limitations and the deterministic summary remain authoritative. AI failure does not discard results. Technical scores are classifier diagnostics, never encounter percentages.
