    Plan a wildlife visit with evidence

    Species Tracker connects a place and a date with recorded wildlife sightings and environmental conditions. It helps you identify animals to look for and compare modeled conditions for a selected species.

    Three focused questions

    1. What animals have been recorded here in this season?
    2. How do conditions look for one animal?
    3. Which supported upcoming day has the most favorable modeled conditions?

    What the evidence can say

    Community sightings are observations, not surveys of every animal. More records can mean more observers, easier access, or easier identification. A classifier trained on sightings and background samples does not establish the probability that you will see an animal.

    This portfolio project demonstrates GIS, data ingestion, model validation, asynchronous APIs, and an accessible web interface. It does not provide navigation or field-safety advice.

    How to use Species Tracker

    Start with an area to discover animals, or choose an animal to examine local evidence. Results help you plan a visit; they do not predict the chance of an encounter.

    Find animals in an area

    1. Open Animals nearby and choose Draw area. Add at least three points on the map, then click the first point or press Enter to close the area. Escape cancels drawing; Clear area removes the completed polygon.
    2. Choose your visit date and, optionally, an animal group. Select Find up to 10 animals.
    3. Read the sightings, distinct dates, years, and latest sighting for each animal. Choose Examine this animal to transfer its identity and visit date into Species outlook.

    The list shows the most recorded wild-animal species inside your area during the selected month, using the preceding ten years of available iNaturalist records ending yesterday. For example, two dates in October use the same seasonal evidence. Weather is shown separately at a point inside the area and does not change the ranking.

    Only research-grade, georeferenced, non-captive records are included. Polygon edges count as inside; subspecies are combined under their species. Ties are resolved by distinct observation dates, then taxon ID. If the complete query is too large, narrow the area. Fewer than ten results, or no results, means limited recorded evidence, not an absence of animals.

    Examine one animal

    1. Open Species outlook, type a name, and choose an autocomplete result. You can use the arrow keys and Enter to select it.
    2. Choose Terrestrial or Marine for the setting you want to study. The app does not infer the setting from the name.
    3. Pick a location on the map or enter coordinates, choose a date and analysis, then select Examine evidence.

    Detailed analysis uses a study circle around your selected point. It starts at 5 km and expands to at most 50 km when records are sparse. After selecting an animal from discovery, this circle is centered on a point inside your polygon; it is a different study extent from the discovery area.

    • Historical profile: monthly sightings and years represented in the retrieved local sample. The sample is capped at your Maximum sightings setting (200 by default), so counts may not be complete totals.
    • Environmental outlook: compares forecast conditions with conditions associated with local sightings and generated background samples. Background samples describe available conditions; they are not confirmed animal absences.
    • Key factors: shows how much each variable contributes to a Random Forest classifier. Importance percentages describe the model, not encounter chances or ecological causes. This analysis describes historical associations rather than your selected day's forecast.

    Compare upcoming days

    After an Environmental outlook produces a usable model, select When should I go? to compare the next seven days starting today. This uses the same animal, study circle, environmental setting, and model as your analysis.

    A ranking appears only when spatial and chronological validation both outperform a seasonality-only baseline, with at least two complete days inside the training ranges and differing scores. Unsupported days are excluded. If those checks fail, the app shows conditions and explains why a reliable ranking is unavailable.

    Read the evidence and limitations

    Terrestrial analysis uses air temperature, wind speed, and rainfall. Marine analysis uses sea surface temperature, wind speed, wave height, and current velocity. Both include cyclic seasonality. Values are daily UTC averages, except rainfall, which is a daily total; all 24 hourly values are required.

    Forecasts depend on source coverage. For an Environmental outlook outside the supported forecast window, the app shows the historical profile with weather unavailable. Missing environmental records are excluded rather than replaced. Coastal cells and coarse data resolution can limit marine analysis.

    Check Evidence used for the study radius, sightings, date coverage, background samples, and exclusions. Expand Technical evidence, validation, and relative scores for model diagnostics and feature units. Results are marked experimental when either validation holdout cannot be evaluated.

    More sightings can mean more observers, easier access, or easier identification. Neither the seasonal list nor classifier scores establish how likely you are to see an animal. Optional AI explanations summarize the evidence; if AI is unavailable, a deterministic explanation remains available.

    Saved choices and recovery

    This browser remembers your last selected animal and environmental setting. Refreshing the same tab resumes an active task. Tasks are held in server memory for 24 hours after completion; a server restart removes them, so you may need to run the request again.

    A completed polygon stays while you work until you clear or replace it. Refresh restores it when resuming a discovery task; it is not saved permanently. Drawing requires map interaction; species analysis also supports typed coordinates.

    Queries send the selected location or area to the data services. Optional AI summaries receive the animal name and structured model evidence, without exact coordinates or source-record identifiers. See Resources for data sources and credits.
